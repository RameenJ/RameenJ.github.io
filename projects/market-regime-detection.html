Macro-Market Regime Detection System

A quant-research-style system that classifies the US macro-financial environment into four interpretable regimes — Risk-On, Risk-Off, High-Volatility, Inflation-Driven — using unsupervised machine learning on 16 engineered macro and market features, refreshed automatically every trading day.

The engineering behind it

  • Automated, scheduled inference — a GitHub Actions workflow runs every weekday, pulls new data, re-engineers features, and re-decodes the regime sequence, then commits the updated artifacts and triggers a redeploy.
  • Frozen model, deliberate re-fit policy — the fitted PCA and HMM are versioned and reused for inference so regime boundaries stay stable; periodic manual re-fitting is a design decision, not an oversight.
  • Train/serve consistency — new data is z-scored with stored training-time means and standard deviations, so live features match what the model was fit on.
  • Leakage discipline — monthly macro series are forward-filled only, and backtest regime labels are lagged one day, so nothing in the pipeline sees information from the future.
  • Explainable output — every predicted regime ships with feature-contribution scores and a plain-English explanation, and a refresh log records each run.

Why regime detection, not price forecasting

Most machine-learning-in-finance projects try to predict tomorrow's price. This system asks a different question: what kind of market environment is this, right now? Asset-class behaviour, risk premia, and strategy performance all vary systematically across macro regimes — the same equity strategy that thrives when markets are Risk-On can destroy capital in a High-Volatility regime. Regime classification is closer to how professional macro and quant researchers actually frame market analysis: not "will the market rise tomorrow," but "what is the governing regime, and how should positioning reflect it?"

The four regimes

  • Risk-On — low volatility, tight credit spreads, an expanding economy, healthy sentiment. Observed 26.6% of the sample.
  • Risk-Off — elevated caution, below-average inflation, wider spreads. The most persistent regime, at 41.0% of the sample.
  • High-Volatility — crisis and acute stress: the 2008 financial crisis, the 2020 COVID crash. 14.6% of the sample.
  • Inflation-Driven — elevated price pressure and rising breakeven inflation, characteristic of 2021–2023. 17.8% of the sample.

The model was validated against four known macro events before deployment — the COVID crash (Mar 2020), GFC peak stress (Oct 2008), the 2022 inflation peak, and a 2018 bull-market stretch — and correctly labelled all four.

Pipeline

01
Data collection & alignment
Daily market data and monthly macro series, forward-filled onto the trading-day calendar so the model only ever sees what a real participant would know on that day.
02
Feature engineering
16 features spanning returns, rolling and GARCH volatility, drawdown, yield-curve slope, credit spreads, CPI, and a PMI proxy — all z-scored on training statistics.
03
Modelling
PCA to 4 components (74.3% variance) → K-means as an independent cross-check → a full-covariance Gaussian HMM with 20 random restarts → Viterbi decoding of the regime sequence.
04
Explanation engine
Feature-contribution scoring and plain-English narrative generation — so a labelled regime always comes with a "why."
05
Dashboard
A four-tab Streamlit app: Timeline, Current Regime, Statistics, and a regime-aware Backtest against a static 60/40 benchmark.
06
Daily refresh
A GitHub Actions workflow pulls new data, re-engineers features, and re-runs Viterbi decoding on the frozen model every weekday — regime boundaries stay stable rather than being re-fit daily.

Modelling notes

A Gaussian HMM with four hidden states and full covariance matrices is the primary regime engine — full covariance lets each state capture within-regime correlation, such as VIX and credit spreads rising together in a Risk-Off state. All diagonal transition probabilities exceed 0.98: once the market enters a regime, it strongly tends to stay there, the expected signature of a genuine macro regime rather than a noisy day-to-day signal.

An independent K-means cross-check on the same PCA-reduced features agrees with the HMM sequence 80.3% of the time, via Hungarian-algorithm state matching. Most of the remaining disagreement sits in the Inflation-Driven state, where the HMM's temporal memory adds real value over K-means' memoryless assignment. A GARCH(1,1) model with Student-t innovations supplies a model-based volatility estimate alongside simple rolling realized volatility, to capture volatility clustering that a rolling standard deviation misses.

Two data-sourcing substitutions worth noting

Two standard series used in most regime models were not freely available and required substitution — a real-world constraint that doesn't show up in textbooks. The ICE BofA US High-Yield Index spread was restricted by FRED to a rolling three-year window in April 2026; Moody's Baa–10Y corporate spread stands in, capturing the same credit-risk-premium signal with full free history. The ISM Manufacturing PMI has been licensed through Bloomberg since 2016; the Philadelphia Fed Manufacturing Index substitutes, using the same diffusion-index methodology and closely tracking the ISM release in practice.

Backtest & limitations

A simple regime-conditional allocation (85/15 equity/bond in Risk-On, tapering to 20/80 in High-Volatility) is compared against a static 60/40 benchmark, with regime labels lagged one day before computing returns to avoid look-ahead bias. The backtest doesn't model transaction costs, slippage, or rebalancing friction, and is illustrative of the regime framework's logic rather than a tradeable strategy. The HMM is re-fit periodically rather than continuously, and the current build covers US equities and macro data only — a euro-area extension is future work.
