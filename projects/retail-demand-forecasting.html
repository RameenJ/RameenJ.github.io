Retail Demand Forecasting & Inventory Intelligence

An end-to-end decision-support platform that turns retail sales history into demand forecasts, calibrated uncertainty, pricing insight, and actionable replenishment recommendations — built on the premise that a forecast only matters if it improves a decision.

The engineering behind it

  • One domain package, many interfaces — forecasting and inventory logic live in a shared Python package consumed by the Streamlit dashboard, a documented FastAPI service, and a tool-backed analytics assistant, so behaviour is never duplicated across layers.
  • Tool-calling analytics assistant — natural-language questions are answered through explicit tools over the forecasting and inventory modules, so answers trace back to real computed evidence.
  • Tested end to end — unit tests cover features, leakage controls, backtests, interval calibration, and inventory simulation; integration tests cover dashboard startup and the full workflow.
  • Reproducible by design — deterministic demo mode, recorded seeds and configuration, and identical forecast origins and horizons when comparing models.
  • Evaluated on outcomes — models are judged against baselines and by downstream inventory results (stockouts, excess, service level), not forecast error alone.

Why this exists

Retail forecasting is only useful if it improves a decision. Predicting next week's unit sales isn't enough on its own — planners also need to know how uncertain that prediction is, which SKUs need attention, how much inventory to hold, and how price or policy changes might change the outcome. This platform connects those pieces into one reproducible workflow: sales data → leakage-safe features → model backtesting → calibrated forecasts → inventory policy simulation → business recommendations.

What it delivers

  • Demand forecasting — store–SKU forecasts from simple baselines, a global XGBoost model, and an optional Prophet benchmark.
  • Honest evaluation — rolling-origin backtesting that preserves time order, so no future information leaks into training.
  • Forecast uncertainty — residual-calibrated prediction intervals for risk-aware planning, instead of unsupported point estimates.
  • Portfolio segmentation — ABC/XYZ classification separating high-value items from stable, variable, and intermittent demand.
  • Price intelligence — hierarchical price-elasticity estimation, with fallbacks when a single series lacks enough evidence.
  • Inventory decisions — reorder recommendations informed by forecast demand, lead time, uncertainty, and service-level targets.
  • Policy evaluation — a sequential inventory simulation measuring stockouts, excess inventory, and service level over time.
  • Scenario analysis — what-if controls for demand, price, lead-time, and inventory-policy changes.

Methodology

01
Data & feature engineering
A deterministic demo pipeline plus sampled ingestion from the M5 Forecasting dataset; time-aware lag, rolling, calendar, and price features with strict leakage controls, and ABC/XYZ portfolio segmentation up front.
02
Forecasting
Four simple baselines are benchmarked before accepting model complexity; a global XGBoost model shares signal across related store–SKU series, with Prophet as an optional statistical benchmark. Evaluation is rolling-origin, and intervals are calibrated from out-of-sample residuals.
03
Econometrics & scenarios
STL decomposition separates trend, seasonality, and residual variation; price elasticity is estimated at the most specific reliable level and falls back through a hierarchy when a series lacks price variation. Scenario controls keep model forecasts distinguishable from explicit business assumptions.
04
Inventory intelligence
Forecast distributions become reorder-oriented recommendations that account for lead time, on-hand inventory, service targets, and demand uncertainty — evaluated by a sequential simulator that never uses future realized demand.

Design principles

  • Decision-first — every analytical component feeds a planning or replenishment decision.
  • Time-aware — training, evaluation, features, and simulation all respect chronological order.
  • Baseline-driven — complex models have to demonstrate value over simple alternatives.
  • Uncertainty-aware — recommendations use prediction risk, not just point forecasts.
  • Reproducible — demo runs are deterministic; core behaviour is covered by unit and integration tests.
  • Interface-independent — forecasting and inventory logic are shared cleanly across Python, FastAPI, and Streamlit.

Dashboard workflow

The five-page Streamlit dashboard is organised around the questions a retail planner actually asks: what is happening (portfolio KPIs and segmentation), what is likely to happen next (forecasts and uncertainty), which products need action (stockout and overstock risk), what if assumptions change (scenario controls), and why is this recommendation being made (diagnostics and a tool-backed analytics assistant that traces supporting evidence).

Limitations

This is a decision-support MVP, not an autonomous ordering system. Results from sampled or synthetic data shouldn't be read as production business impact, and price-elasticity estimates are observational rather than causal without a proper experimental design. Forecast quality can deteriorate under assortment changes, supply constraints, or unrepresented promotions. Production deployment would additionally need authentication, persistent storage, monitoring, and retailer-specific validation.
